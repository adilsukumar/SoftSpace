# Remaining validation methods

These are proposed internal development methods, not completed tests, product standards or certification. Loads, cycle counts and comparison limits must be agreed for the final design. Run mechanical overload and environmental tests on fixtures, not while worn.

| Test | Proposed method and output | Needed before a meaningful result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Band material | Measure bending force and displacement, unload and record permanent set; repeat in the intended print or mould direction, humidity and temperature. Fit the actual constitutive model. | Grade, temper, manufacturing direction, coupon dimensions, equipment and samples. |
| Foam | Record loading and unloading stress versus strain at several rates and temperatures; include recovery versus time. Compare at the same thickness and area. | Exact foam grade and density, cut or moulded samples, compression tester and conditioning. |
| Cover | Measure stretch and recovery in both directions, surface friction, rub noise, seam failure and changes after cleaning. | Actual laminate, textile, seam and bond. Film vapour transmission is not the same as air or sound transmission. |
| Joint and latch stiffness | Use the final pin, bearing, angular stop and latch. Measure load versus rotation, free play and release force. Analyse the measured geometry with contact and final material curves. | Functional joint geometry and material/friction inputs. The current pin and retaining-cap placeholders are insufficient. |
| Axial locking and stop | Ramp pull force in a fixture; record slip, stop engagement, damage and residual deformation. Hold loads to observe creep. Suggested comparison levels 2, 5, 10 and 20 N are study inputs, not guaranteed ratings. | Real screw/insert, torque, friction, stop geometry and material behaviour. |
| Repeated folding | Log fold torque, free play, cracks and fastener loosening at 100, 1000 and 10000 cycles as provisional checkpoints. Recheck the worn latch and seal afterward. | Final joint and physical prototype; cycles have not been performed. |
| Band fatigue | Repeatedly spread to measured worn widths; record force drift and damage. Include the largest intended head fit without relying on one synthetic head. | Final spring section, material fatigue data and fixture. |
| Drop and transport | A possible internal programme is 1 m fixture-controlled drops over six orientations, plus 25, 50 and 100 N packed-compression comparisons. Record cracks, joint damage and function after each event. These are proposals, not passed requirements. | Finished mass distribution, case/pack configuration, contact surfaces and physical units. An energy calculation alone is not a drop test. |
| Worn contact | Calibrate pressure mapping; compare uniform and soft-zone pads with the same head, glasses position, band force and cover. Report distributed pressure and fit-to-fit variation. | Measured geometry and materials; nonlinear foam, cover and frictional contact model. |
| Heat and moisture | Compare prototypes and original pads on a controlled fixture at stated ambient conditions and duration. Measure temperature, humidity, moisture gain and recovery after removal. | Actual material transport data and a sample. Earlier saturation cases remain unresolved. |
| Incoming noise | Use the same sound field and measurement fixture, repeated seating, several glasses arms, and original/uniform/soft-zone pads. Report frequency-dependent differences and repeatability. | Exact Sony mounting geometry or standalone cup/seal geometry, calibrated acoustic equipment and prototypes. |
| Outgoing playback | For Sony only, match measured sound level inside the cup rather than just the volume-slider setting. Measure outside at fixed positions and distances; repeat fitting. | Working compatible pads and headphone. Passive defender has no playback driver. |
| Frequency response and ANC | Compare original and replacement pads on the same headphone, fixture and fit; ANC off and on. Report bass, response and repeatability without claiming zero change. | Sony pad and groove measurements and compatible samples. |
| Wearer acceptance | Voluntary feedback on pressure, texture, heat, sound manageability, removal and willingness to wear again. Stop on request or discomfort. Seek children's assent and appropriate consent. Avoid intentionally distressing sound exposure. | Prototype that has passed preliminary fit and mechanical checks; actual users. Simulation cannot establish individual preference. |
| Cost and manufacturing | Obtain quotes for chosen grades, processes, tooling, labour, scrap and replacement parts. Measure dimensional variation and assembly yield. | Suppliers, quantities and samples. No lowest-price claim follows from this study. |

## Data to retain

Keep raw force/displacement and acoustic records, specimen and CAD identifiers, material batch, conditioning, equipment calibration, test settings, repeated trials, photographs, failures and the before/after functional check. Define acceptance criteria before comparing prototypes. Update both Word reports with results and uncertainty rather than changing a planned test to a pass.
